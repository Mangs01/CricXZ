CHRISTCHURCH TEST • DAY 1 REPORT

New Zealand Recover to 246/5 as Blundell, Phillips Resist India in Christchurch

Blundell and Phillips hold firm after India take three wickets without conceding a run.

28 November 2026CricXZ Sports DeskDay 1 Report

AI-generated CricXZ editorial illustration; not an actual match photograph. Artwork illustrates the Day 1 report.

New Zealand ended the opening day of the second Test against India on 246/5 at Hagley Oval. Tom Blundell was unbeaten on 53 and Glenn Phillips on 36 after Tom Latham’s 59 helped the hosts negotiate a day interrupted by a sharp Indian breakthrough.

Day 1 Stumps — 27 November 2026

New Zealand: 246/5 in 85 overs
India: Yet to bat
Venue: Hagley Oval, Christchurch
Toss: India chose to field.

Published 28 November. This is the completed Day 1 snapshot, not a live score or a Day 2 report.

India Turn 110/1 into 110/4

Latham’s innings ended when Akash Deep had him caught behind. Anshul Kamboj then dismissed Henry Nicholls for 16, before Akash removed Rachin Ravindra without scoring. Those three wickets fell with the total unchanged at 110, transforming the shape of the innings.

Akash finished the day with 2/52. Gurnoor Brar, Kamboj and Washington Sundar each took one wicket; Jasprit Bumrah returned 0/37.

Blundell and Phillips Keep New Zealand in the Contest

Daryl Mitchell made 31 before Sundar dismissed him at 184/5. Blundell and Phillips then added an unbroken 62, taking the hosts through to stumps. Their stand meant India could not turn the middle-session burst into a completed innings on the first day.

CricXZ Analysis: A Burst Without a Finish

The unchanged total across three dismissals is the clearest measure of India’s opportunity. It removed an established opener and exposed the middle order in quick succession. Yet the last five wickets were still intact at the close: the recovery after that burst matters as much as the burst itself when assessing the day.

For New Zealand, the sixth-wicket stand changed the immediate problem. At five down, the priority was to protect the remaining batting while adding runs; by stumps, two set batters were still available to continue. For India, the corresponding task was to break that partnership before the remaining wickets could contribute further.

Neither task establishes who will win. India had not begun their reply, so the scoreboard did not yet show how their batters would handle the same contest. This assessment is CricXZ’s reading of the recorded innings position, rather than a prediction of the result.
