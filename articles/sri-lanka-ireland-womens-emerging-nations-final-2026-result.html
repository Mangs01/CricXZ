Sri Lanka Win Women’s Emerging Nations Trophy; Sandeepani and Dulani Star

Sathya Sandeepani’s four wickets and Imesha Dulani’s unbeaten 44 sealed a six-wicket victory in Ajman.

25 November 2026CricXZ Sports DeskWomen’s Final Report

AI-generated CricXZ editorial illustration; not an actual match photograph. Artwork illustrates the title-winning result.

Sri Lanka won the ICC Women’s Emerging Nations Trophy 2026 with a six-wicket victory over Ireland at Karwan Sports Club in Ajman on 25 November. They finished the competition unbeaten, winning all eight matches.

Final Score

Ireland: 96 all out in 19.3 overs
Sri Lanka: 99/4 in 12.4 overs
Result: Sri Lanka won by six wickets, with 44 balls remaining
Key performances: Sathya Sandeepani 4/13; Imesha Dulani 44* off 25.

Sandeepani Gives Sri Lanka the Early Advantage

After Sri Lanka chose to field, Sandeepani removed Gaby Lewis and Lara Maritz to leave Ireland 10/2. Ireland then slipped to 54/6, putting the lower order under pressure to rebuild.

Alana Dalzell’s 24 was Ireland’s highest score. Sandeepani finished with four wickets for 13 runs from four overs as Ireland were dismissed for 96.

Ireland Strike Back Before Dulani Settles the Chase

Sanjana Kavindi made 25, but Kia McCartney dismissed her and captain Harshitha Samarawickrama from consecutive deliveries. Cara Murray also took two wickets, and Sri Lanka reached 69/4.

Dulani remained unbeaten on 44 from 25 balls, including four fours and two sixes. Kavisha Dilhari contributed 16 not out as their unbroken 30-run partnership carried Sri Lanka to 99/4 in 12.4 overs.

Eight Wins Complete an Unbeaten Campaign

The final added the eighth win to Sri Lanka’s perfect tournament record. Ireland’s three Super Six victories had taken them into the title match, but they could not prevent Sri Lanka completing the unbeaten run.

CricXZ Analysis: Wickets Created Time for the Chase

The bowling performance shaped the match before Sri Lanka began batting. With a modest target, losing wickets did not automatically require a frantic response. The batting side could weigh the remaining runs against the time available.

Ireland still created a genuine opening through consecutive wickets and a further middle-order breakthrough. The unbeaten finishing partnership mattered because it stopped that pressure from developing into a deeper collapse. This section is CricXZ editorial analysis.
