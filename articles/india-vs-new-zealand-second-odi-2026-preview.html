India vs New Zealand 2nd ODI Preview: Conway Out, Kelly Called Up for Wellington

India take a 1-0 lead to Wellington as New Zealand adjust to Conway’s series absence. Kelly joins the hosts, while Henry has been cleared of concussion.

Published 6 November 2026CricXZ Sports DeskMatch Preview • Wellington

India face New Zealand in the second ODI at Hnry Stadium, Wellington, on Saturday, 7 November 2026. The scheduled start is 7:30 AM IST, or 3:00 PM local time. India lead the five-match series 1-0 after their rain-affected Auckland victory.

Venue: Hnry Stadium, Wellington
Scheduled start: 7:30 AM IST / 3:00 PM NZDT / 02:00 UTC
Series: India lead 1-0

Conway Out of Remaining ODIs; Kelly Joins

New Zealand Cricket confirmed on 6 November that Devon Conway will miss the remaining ODIs after the concussion he sustained in a training collision earlier in the week. His absence now extends beyond the Auckland opener.

Nick Kelly, originally selected for the fourth and fifth ODIs, has been called into the squad in Wellington as Conway’s replacement. NZC also confirmed that Matt Henry, involved in the same fielding incident, has been cleared of concussion. That medical update should not be confused with a confirmed place in the playing XI.

India Bring an Early Advantage from Auckland

India won the first ODI by five wickets under the DLS method. New Zealand were dismissed for 185, and India reached a revised target of 143 with Virat Kohli making 74 from 51 balls. Shubman Gill contributed 37.

Arshdeep Singh’s four wickets and Gurnoor Brar’s three supplied the breakthroughs, while Player of the Match Anshul Kamboj conceded only 22 runs in ten overs and took two wickets. Read the full Auckland result and DLS explanation.

Selection Questions Before Wellington

India have a useful decision to make about continuity. Their opening combination produced a win, and the younger seamers contributed alongside Arshdeep. The question is how the team balances that performance against the alternatives available in the squad. No change should be treated as settled before an official announcement.

For New Zealand, Kelly’s arrival expands the batting options after Conway’s withdrawal. Henry’s clearance is a separate development affecting the bowling group. The toss will clarify how both updates translate into the final side.

CricXZ View: Three Things to Watch

  • New Zealand’s partnerships: The hosts need a more sustained batting platform than the total they produced in Auckland. Reducing the number of rebuilding phases would give the middle order greater freedom.
  • India’s bowling roles: Wicket-taking and economy complemented each other in the opener. Whether India can repeat that balance is more useful to watch than assuming the leading wicket-taker must provide every decisive spell.
  • Batting beyond the main partnership: Kohli and Gill built the first chase around a substantial stand. India will want contributions through the order, particularly if the next chase or first-innings total demands a longer effort.
