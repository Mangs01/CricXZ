India vs New Zealand 3rd ODI Preview: India Eye Series Win in Hamilton

India arrive in Hamilton one win from sealing the five-match ODI series. New Zealand must respond with a reshaped pace group after their latest squad update.

Published 9 November 2026CricXZ Sports DeskMatch Preview • Hamilton

AI-generated CricXZ editorial illustration; not an actual match or training photograph.

India have the series advantage, but Wellington showed why a 2-0 lead should not be confused with an easy assignment. The third ODI at Seddon Park gives Shubman Gill’s side a chance to clinch the series, while New Zealand need a win to keep it alive. The hosts’ changing bowling availability adds a fresh selection question before Tuesday’s game.

Match Details: Hamilton Third ODI

  • Match: New Zealand vs India, third ODI of five.
  • Date: Tuesday, 10 November 2026.
  • Venue: Seddon Park, Hamilton.
  • Scheduled start: 2:00 AM UTC / 7:30 AM IST / 3:00 PM NZDT.
  • Series situation: India lead 2-0.

The start time comes from the BCCI match centre. Scheduled times remain subject to official changes; the toss will determine the confirmed playing XIs.

What Wellington Changed

India won the second ODI by four runs under the DLS method. Gurnoor Brar returned 4/30 as New Zealand were dismissed for 134. The chase still tested India: Shreyas Iyer made 30 and KL Rahul finished unbeaten on 26 before rain brought the contest to an early end.

That result gives the Hamilton preview two distinct threads. India’s bowling has helped create the series lead, while the batting group has reason to seek a more secure response when wickets fall early. New Zealand, meanwhile, can take encouragement from putting the visitors under pressure despite defending a modest total.

New Zealand’s Latest Bowling Availability

NZC’s 9 November update brings Kristian Clarke into the ODI squad as cover for Blair Tickner. Although Tickner has been cleared of concussion, his replacement in the Wellington XI means he must observe the mandatory seven-day stand-down period.

Matt Henry is absent from the third-match squad after reassessment following his training collision with Devon Conway. A fourth-ODI return is the target, rather than a confirmed Hamilton appearance. Lockie Ferguson has also been ruled out of ODIs four and five as he continues recovering from a low-grade right-calf tear.

Kyle Jamieson and Jacob Duffy remain among the options named for game three. Squad inclusion alone does not establish the final bowling combination, so Clarke’s call-up should not be described as a guaranteed debut.

India’s Selection Context

BCCI added Auqib Nabi to the squad for the first three ODIs on 31 October. Gill remains the designated captain for this part of the series, with Shreyas Iyer as vice-captain. Nitish Kumar Reddy’s availability in that announcement was limited to the opening ODI.

For India, the editorial question is how to balance the attack’s recent success with the resources available for Hamilton. That is a selection question, not evidence that any particular change has been decided. This preview does not publish an unconfirmed XI as an official team.

CricXZ View: Three Questions That Could Shape the Match

  • Can India reduce the cost of early wickets? Keeping more batting options available through the middle of the innings would make the chase or a first-innings build less dependent on a rescue partnership.
  • Can New Zealand turn resistance into a larger platform? A sustained partnership gives a batting side more choices about when to accelerate. Repeated rebuilding phases make those decisions harder.
  • How will the hosts organise their pace roles? The useful comparison is between new-ball responsibility, middle-over pressure and closing overs, rather than assuming a replacement must duplicate an unavailable player’s role.

These are CricXZ’s analytical priorities, not a pitch report or a numerical win forecast. We have not verified a current pitch inspection or match-day weather forecast and therefore do not assign a par score, promise uninterrupted play or claim a toss advantage.

What a Win Would Mean

A third victory would give India an unassailable lead with two matches remaining. A New Zealand win would reduce the deficit to 2-1 and extend the contest. Readers should distinguish those possible outcomes from an actual result: this is a pre-match article.
