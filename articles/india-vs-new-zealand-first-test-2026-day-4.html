NEW ZEALAND vs INDIA 2026 • 1ST TEST • DAY 4

Pant 128 Powers India to Record 720/9; New Zealand Face Final-Day Survival Test

Pant's century and Jurel's 78 lifted India to a record total before Conway and Ravindra kept New Zealand's draw hopes alive.

22 November 2026CricXZ Sports DeskDay 4 Stumps Report

AI-generated CricXZ editorial illustration; not an actual match photograph. Scores show Day 4 stumps.

India declared on 720/9 before New Zealand reached 119/2 at stumps on Day 4 of the first Test in Wellington on 22 November 2026. Rishabh Pant scored 128 and Dhruv Jurel added 78 as India built a first-innings lead of 352. The hosts ended the day 233 runs behind, with eight wickets remaining. The Test is still ongoing.

Wellington Test: Day 4 Snapshot

Venue: Basin Reserve, Wellington
New Zealand: 368 & 119/2 (45 overs)
India: 720/9 declared
Position: New Zealand trail by 233 runs
Unbeaten batters: Devon Conway 59*, Rachin Ravindra 27*
Status: Day 4 stumps; no final result.

Pant Turns India's Advantage Into a Record Total

India resumed on 486/4, already 118 ahead after KL Rahul's 136 on Day 3. Nightwatchman Anshul Kamboj contributed 38, while Pant accelerated the innings with his ninth Test century.

Pant reached his hundred with a six and finished with 128. Jurel followed with a run-a-ball 78, helping India add 234 runs during the day before the declaration. The visitors' 720/9 was their highest overseas Test total and the highest Test innings total by any team in New Zealand, according to the ICC.

A 352-Run Lead, Then Two Early Openings

The declaration left New Zealand needing 352 simply to make India bat again. Jasprit Bumrah removed Tom Latham for a duck in the opening over, completing a pair for the New Zealand captain.

Henry Nicholls, who had made 175 not out in the first innings, scored 31 before Prasidh Krishna dismissed him, with Jurel taking the catch at short leg. That left New Zealand dependent on another substantial partnership.

Conway and Ravindra Keep the Contest Alive

Conway reached stumps on 59 not out from 137 balls, alongside Ravindra on 27 not out from 72. New Zealand negotiated 45 overs to close on 119/2.

The hosts remain well behind, but India still need eight wickets. The surviving pair's resistance means the final day is a contest between India's search for breakthroughs and New Zealand's attempt to use up the remaining time.

CricXZ Analysis: Runs Have Bought Time, Not a Result

India's declaration created a large cushion against having to chase. That allows the bowlers to attack without an immediate scoring threat from New Zealand. However, a big lead alone cannot produce a win: wickets are the remaining requirement.

Conway and Ravindra showed that survival is possible on this surface. For India, maintaining pressure through consecutive spells will matter more than the size of the scoreboard. For New Zealand, a long partnership can take time away from the visitors even without rapid scoring. This is CricXZ editorial analysis, not a prediction or a confirmed result.

What to Watch on Day 5

India will seek early wickets to expose the middle order. New Zealand will look to extend the Conway-Ravindra stand and reduce the time available to take eight wickets. This article records Day 4 stumps only; a final result requires a separate verified update.
