Why Mohammed Siraj Missed the Wellington Test: Shubman Gill Explains

Gill explained the difficult selection call after Wellington, with India's next combination still subject to conditions in Christchurch.

23 November 2026CricXZ Sports DeskTeam Selection Update

AI-generated CricXZ editorial illustration; not an actual match photograph. Artwork illustrates the selection story.

Shubman Gill has said current bowling form shaped India's decision to leave Mohammed Siraj out of the first Test against New Zealand in Wellington. Speaking after the drawn match on 23 November 2026, the captain described the call as difficult and said he had informed Siraj before the game.

The Selection Update

Confirmed: Siraj missed the Wellington playing XI.
Gill's explanation: Current form and which bowlers were performing best.
Next match: Second Test in Christchurch, starting 27 November.
Second-Test XI: Not confirmed by the comments reported here.

Gill Explains the Decision

In comments carried by the ICC, Gill praised Siraj's contribution to India while explaining that selection required a judgement about the bowlers in the best current form. He also acknowledged their long association and said leaving Siraj out had been personally difficult.

Gill said he had discussed the decision with the fast bowler before the Test. The explanation concerns the choice of playing XI for Wellington; it should not be read as a permanent decision about Siraj's place in India's plans.

India Chose Four Other Pace Options

India fielded Jasprit Bumrah, Akash Deep, Prasidh Krishna and Anshul Kamboj as their pace attack, with Washington Sundar providing the specialist spin option. Siraj and Ravindra Jadeja were outside the XI.

The selection came back into focus when New Zealand held on for a draw. For the match details, read our Wellington first-Test final report.

What Gill Said About the Second Test

Gill indicated that he expected most of the team to remain the same, while leaving the final combination dependent on the pitch. He also backed the effort of the players who had taken the field.

That is an indication of possible continuity, rather than an announced XI. It neither confirms a Siraj return nor establishes that he will miss the Christchurch Test.

Squad Selection and Playing-XI Selection Are Different

The BCCI's original Test squad announcement included Siraj. A touring squad gives the team options; only eleven players can start a Test. Missing one match therefore does not by itself mean a player has been removed from the squad.

For the next Test, the meaningful update will be the confirmed combination and the conditions the team encounters. Until then, predicted changes should be labelled as predictions.

CricXZ Analysis: Keep the Explanation and the Outcome Separate

Gill's comments tell us why the team made its selection. They cannot establish what the result would have been with a different bowler. Saying Siraj would certainly have turned the draw into a win would go beyond the evidence.

The question for Christchurch is how India balance the attack for that surface. The captain's preference for continuity leaves room for assessment, while Siraj remains part of the selection discussion. This section is CricXZ editorial analysis.

When Is the Next Test?

The BCCI schedule lists the second Test in Christchurch from 27 November 2026, with a scheduled start of 3:30 AM IST. The series remains level at 0-0 after Wellington.
