Hardik Pandya to PBKS, Arshdeep Singh to MI: What the IPL 2027 Trade Changes

Hardik Pandya moves to Punjab Kings and Arshdeep Singh joins Mumbai Indians. Read the confirmed fees, official captaincy position and CricXZ team-balance analysis.

Published 14 November 2026CricXZ Sports DeskIPL Trade Analysis

AI-generated CricXZ editorial illustration in franchise-inspired colours; not a transfer photograph or an official 2027 kit reveal.

Hardik Pandya will represent Punjab Kings and Arshdeep Singh will join Mumbai Indians ahead of IPL 2027. The IPL confirmed the exchange on 11 November 2026, with both franchises announcing the moves. This explainer, published on 14 November, examines the confirmed terms and the different roles each team must now balance.

The Confirmed Trade at a Glance

  • Hardik Pandya: Mumbai Indians to Punjab Kings.
  • Arshdeep Singh: Punjab Kings to Mumbai Indians.
  • Hardik's listed fee: ₹18 crore, up from ₹16.35 crore.
  • Arshdeep's listed fee: ₹18 crore, unchanged.
  • Official announcement: 11 November 2026, ahead of IPL 2027.

These are the player fees stated in the IPL advisory. They should not be presented as an auction price or as proof of a separate cash payment between the franchises.

What Punjab Kings Gain

Punjab's announcement emphasises Hardik's experience and his ability to affect matches with both bat and ball. The franchise welcomed him while thanking Arshdeep for his contribution to the team.

As recorded in that announcement, Hardik had 2,955 runs and 82 wickets across 162 IPL appearances. Those figures are the franchise's announcement-date totals, rather than a live career counter.

CricXZ analysis: A seam-bowling all-rounder can create flexibility in a T20 XI. If Hardik supplies useful overs as well as middle-order batting, Punjab can consider combinations that might otherwise require separate batting and bowling places. The value depends on his availability and actual bowling workload, rather than the all-rounder label alone.

The Bowling Role Punjab Must Rebuild

Arshdeep's departure removes a long-established left-arm pace option. Punjab's release records 111 wickets in 96 matches for the franchise as of the trade announcement.

CricXZ analysis: The task is not simply to add another fast bowler. Punjab must decide who takes responsibility with the new ball and who handles the difficult late overs. Hardik can contribute to the attack, but his arrival does not automatically replace every phase of Arshdeep's role.

What Mumbai Indians Gain

Mumbai's statement welcomes Arshdeep's international experience and the extra versatility he brings to the bowling group. It also recognises Hardik's contribution across two spells with the franchise.

CricXZ analysis: Left-arm pace gives a captain different angles against both right- and left-handed batters. Arshdeep's addition can widen Mumbai's options for allocating overs across an innings. Whether that improves results will depend on the complete attack, conditions and execution.

There is a corresponding balance question: losing an established batting all-rounder changes how Mumbai can connect their batting depth to their bowling allocation. One specialist's arrival does not reproduce the departing player's full set of roles.

What Is Confirmed About Captaincy?

Mumbai says it will evaluate its captaincy options and make an announcement at the appropriate time. The trade statement therefore does not name a successor to Hardik.

Punjab's cited acquisition announcement also does not appoint Hardik as captain. His leadership experience is part of his background, not confirmation of a new role. Any subsequent captaincy announcement should be reported separately from this trade.

Why It Is Too Early to Declare a Winner

CricXZ analysis: The teams have exchanged different skill sets. Punjab gain batting-and-bowling flexibility but must rebuild a specialist pace role; Mumbai strengthen a specialist pace option while reassessing all-round balance. Final squad construction, player fitness, selected XIs and match performances will provide the evidence needed to judge the trade.
