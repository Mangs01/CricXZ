India vs New Zealand 4th ODI: Henry Six-For Seals 244-Run Win

Matt Henry took 6/27 and Will Young hit 125 as New Zealand bowled India out for 89 at Bay Oval. India lead the ODI series 2-1 with one match remaining.

Published 13 November 2026CricXZ Sports DeskMatch Report • Mount Maunganui

AI-generated CricXZ editorial illustration; not an actual match or training photograph.

New Zealand kept the ODI series open with a 244-run victory over India at Bay Oval, Mount Maunganui, on 13 November 2026. Will Young made 125 before Matt Henry dismantled the chase with 6/27, leaving India all out for 89.

India vs New Zealand 4th ODI: Result Summary

  • New Zealand: 333 all out in 50 overs.
  • India: 89 all out in 26.4 overs.
  • Result: New Zealand won by 244 runs.
  • Player of the Match: Matt Henry, 6/27.
  • Series: India lead 2-1 with one ODI remaining.

Young Gives New Zealand the Platform

Asked to bat, the hosts found early momentum through Young and Tim Seifert. Their opening partnership reached 85 before Seifert departed for 58 from 48 balls. Young continued to drive the innings, finishing with 125 off 108 deliveries, including 14 fours and four sixes.

Finn Allen contributed 41 from 30 balls. For India, Axar Patel returned 2/39, while Auqib Nabi and Gurnoor Brar also collected two wickets apiece. New Zealand nevertheless used the full innings to reach 333, setting India a target of 334.

Henry Turns the Chase in One Over

India's reply was in crisis almost immediately. Henry dismissed Rohit Sharma, Virat Kohli and stand-in captain Shreyas Iyer for ducks in his opening over. Three senior batters were gone before India had scored a run.

Kyle Jamieson then removed Ruturaj Gaikwad for five. Ishan Kishan and Axar offered resistance, but their recovery could not repair the damage. Kishan's 27 was India's highest score; Axar and Naman Dhir each made 21.

Henry completed his six-wicket haul in 9.4 overs. Matthew Fisher took 3/31 and Jamieson finished with 1/12 as India were dismissed well short of their target.

Why the Opening Powerplay Mattered

New Zealand reached 72 without loss in their first ten overs. India, by contrast, lost three wickets without scoring in the first over. Those starts produced entirely different batting tasks: the hosts could build around a settled opener, while the visitors had to attempt a large chase with much of their experienced top order already dismissed.

The contrast is the clearest explanation of the result. India's later resistance delayed the finish without threatening New Zealand's total.

India Still Lead Ahead of the Final ODI

The final match is scheduled for 15 November at the same venue. India can win the series outright with victory; New Zealand can draw it 2-2. The Hamilton washout remains a no result, so the hosts cannot finish with more wins than India.
