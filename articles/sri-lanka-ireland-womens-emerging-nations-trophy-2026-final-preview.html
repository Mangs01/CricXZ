Sri Lanka vs Ireland Final Set for Women’s Emerging Nations Trophy 2026

Ireland’s 44-run win and Sri Lanka’s four-run success set up a title match in Ajman on 25 November.

Update and Venue Correction — 25 November 2026

Sri Lanka won the final by six wickets. Read the completed-match report. The final ICC report and match record confirm Karwan Sports Club, Ajman. ICC final report and venue confirmation

Sri Lanka and Ireland will contest the ICC Women’s Emerging Nations Trophy 2026 final at Karwan Sports Club in Ajman on Wednesday, 25 November. The line-up was confirmed after Ireland beat the Netherlands by 44 runs and Sri Lanka defeated Pakistan by four runs on 23 November.

Final: Confirmed Details

Fixture: Sri Lanka Women vs Ireland Women
Competition: ICC Women’s Emerging Nations Trophy 2026
Date: Wednesday, 25 November 2026
Venue: Karwan Sports Club, Ajman, UAE
Status: Upcoming final; no result yet.

Maritz and Murray Power Ireland Into the Final

Ireland made 162/3 before dismissing the Netherlands for 118 in 19.3 overs. Lara Maritz finished unbeaten on 57, while Cara Murray returned 5/17 to drive the 44-run victory.

Cricket Ireland named Maritz as Player of the Match and described her innings as her first international half-century. Amy Hunter made 46 and captain Gaby Lewis 37, with the opening pair adding 61 before Maritz helped maintain the momentum.

After Hunter and Louise Little fell off successive deliveries, Abbi Harrison supported Maritz with an unbeaten 16. The late partnership ensured Ireland had a competitive total to defend.

Murray Breaks the Dutch Resistance

Phebe Molkenboer’s 46 and Frederique Overdijk’s 35 gave the Netherlands resistance, but Murray’s leg-spin changed the chase. Cricket Ireland reported that it was her first T20I five-wicket haul.

The Netherlands were unable to rebuild after the middle-order damage. Ireland’s qualification win therefore combined useful top-order contributions, a strong finish with the bat and a decisive wicket-taking spell.

Sri Lanka Hold Off Pakistan

Sri Lanka posted 140/6 and restricted Pakistan to 136/9 to win by four runs. Imesha Dulani led the batting with 56, and Sathya Sandeepani claimed 3/29.

Pakistan threatened a late recovery through Tasmia Rubab, who struck three sixes in an unbeaten 23 from 11 balls. Sri Lanka nevertheless protected their total and enter the final after another close finish.

CricXZ Preview: Two Different Tests of Composure

Ireland’s latest match showed how a side can build an advantage across both innings: establish a batting platform, keep adding runs after wickets, then use a strike bowler to break a chase. Sri Lanka’s narrower victory required them to manage pressure right through the closing stages.

Those are useful recent examples, but they do not establish which side will win the final. Conditions, the selected teams and execution on the day can change the contest. Comparing winning margins from separate matches is not enough to rank the finalists.

Three Things to Watch in the Title Match

  • The opening platform: Early partnerships can give the middle order more freedom to choose when to attack.
  • The response to spin: Batters who rotate the strike can make it harder for a wicket-taking spell to become a collapse.
  • The closing overs: Both retaining wickets and limiting boundary opportunities can influence a tight T20 finish.
